## ASB2014 ground-motion coefficient tables

`engine/gmpe_data.py` carries the coefficient tables of the ground-motion
model of **Akkar, Sandıkkaya & Bommer (2014)**, *Empirical ground-motion
models for point- and extended-source crustal earthquake scenarios in
Europe and the Middle East*, Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering 12(1),
359–387, DOI [10.1007/s10518-013-9461-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10518-013-9461-4).

- The tables were supplied by the authors to the **pyGMM** project on
  2016-03-17 and are reproduced here from that distribution, one table per
  distance metric, with the periods, the per-period coefficients and the
  dispersion terms as published. Nothing in that file is derived from any
  implementation.
- The coefficients are facts of the paper. The functional form in
  `engine/gmpe.py` was written from the paper's equations.
- Copyright in the model and its coefficients remains with the authors.
  PlanX applies the model; it does not claim it.

## pyGMM test fixture (verification data)

`tests/data/asb14_pygmm_reference.json` holds ASB2014 intensity measures
for four scenarios as computed by **pyGMM** — four (magnitude, distance,
Vs30, mechanism) combinations, each evaluated through all three distance
tables, 744 spectral values in total.

- pyGMM: Copyright (c) 2016 Albert Kottke. MIT licence.
  <https://github.com/arkottke/pygmm>
- The file is generated by `scratch/_gen_asb14_reference.py` from pyGMM's
  `tests/data/asb14_tests.json.gz`, which is **not** redistributed here —
  only the scenario parameters and the resulting values are copied.
- It exists so that this engine's implementation can be checked against an
  independent one. The coefficient table is common to both, so what the
  fixture tests is the equations, the site-term branching, the
  per-distance-metric wiring and the PGA/PGV/Sa row indexing — not the
  coefficients.
- pyGMM is **not** a dependency of PlanX and is not imported at runtime or
  in the test suite.

## Hazus fragility curves

`engine/seismic.py` embeds the equivalent-PGA structural fragility curves
of the **Hazus Earthquake Model Technical Manual 6.1** (FEMA, 2022),
Tables 5-37 to 5-40 — median and dispersion per building type, height class
and seismic design level.

- Hazus is published by the United States Federal Emergency Management
  Agency. As a work of the US federal government it is in the public
  domain, and the values are transcribed rather than adapted.
- The design levels are defined against United States construction
  practice. Mapping them onto Turkish regulation years is an uncalibrated
  screening assumption, is documented as such in the engine, the tool's
  help text and the manual, and is not part of any FEMA publication.
